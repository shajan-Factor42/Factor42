Blog/Channels & platforms

When small businesses plan their marketing, they think about search, social, maybe video. One channel that rarely makes the list — but often should — is digital audio: advertising on streaming music services, podcasts, and internet radio. It's overlooked precisely because it's not visual and not flashy, but digital audio reaches people in moments and places no other channel can, often with less competition. Here's why this quiet channel deserves a look.

Factor42 Media4 min read

What digital audio is

Digital audio advertising means ads delivered through the audio people listen to online — streaming music services, podcasts, digital radio. When you hear an ad between songs on a streaming service or during a podcast, that's digital audio advertising. It reaches people through their ears rather than their eyes, in the audio content they're already choosing to listen to.

What makes it distinct is the context of listening. People consume audio in situations where they can't look at a screen — driving, exercising, cooking, working, commuting. Digital audio reaches them in exactly those moments, when other advertising can't get to them at all. That's the channel's quiet superpower: presence in the parts of your customers' day that visual channels simply can't touch.

Why digital audio reaches people everywhere

The great advantage of audio is that people listen while doing other things, which means it reaches them across their whole day, including moments no screen-based channel can:

  • While driving — a captive audience with no screen to compete for attention.
  • While exercising — earbuds in, fully engaged with the audio.
  • While working or doing chores — audio playing in the background of tasks throughout the day.
  • While commuting — time otherwise unreachable by visual advertising.

In all these moments, people are listening but not looking, and digital audio meets them there. It extends your reach into hours of your customers' lives that visual advertising misses entirely. For a business trying to stay present across a customer's whole day, audio fills gaps nothing else can.

Why it's overlooked — and why that's an opportunity

Digital audio gets overlooked mostly because it's not visual and not trendy. It doesn't produce eye-catching creative to admire, it's harder to picture than a display ad or a video, and it's simply not top of mind for most small businesses. So they skip it.

But that very overlooked-ness is an opportunity. Because fewer businesses prioritize digital audio, it can be less crowded and competitive than the channels everyone piles onto — potentially meaning better value and more attention for the businesses that do use it. The channel your competitors ignore is often the one where you can stand out affordably. Digital audio's low profile is exactly why it can be a smart, efficient addition for a business willing to look past the obvious channels.

Making audio work as part of the mix

Like any single channel, digital audio delivers its full value as part of a coordinated marketing effort rather than a standalone experiment. On its own, it reaches people in audio moments — valuable, but limited. Coordinated with your other channels, it becomes more powerful: the awareness it builds during someone's commute or workout can be reinforced when they're later on their phone or computer, and audio fills the gaps in a presence that spans the customer's whole day.

This coordination is natural when your marketing is run as one operation, where digital audio can be added as one channel in a unified plan — reaching people in their listening moments, coordinated with everything else, measured as part of the whole. Rather than a disconnected experiment you're unsure how to evaluate, audio becomes a smart, coordinated extension of your reach. That's how a small business captures the value of this overlooked channel without it becoming another separate thing to manage.

Reach the moments others miss

Digital audio is an overlooked channel that reaches customers in the parts of their day no visual advertising can touch — driving, exercising, working, commuting — often with less competition than the crowded channels everyone else chooses. For a business wanting to stay present across a customer's whole day, and to do it efficiently, audio is worth a serious look.
