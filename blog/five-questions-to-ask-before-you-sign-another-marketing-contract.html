Blog/Choosing a partner

A marketing contract is easy to sign in a hopeful moment. The pitch was good, the rep was likable, the promises were appealing, and you're busy — so you sign, and you find out what you actually agreed to over the months that follow. Sometimes it works out. Often, the things that go wrong were knowable up front, if only the right questions had been asked before the pen touched paper.

Factor42 Media4 min read

Here are five questions worth asking before you sign any marketing contract — agency, freelancer, or otherwise. None require marketing expertise. All of them protect you.

1. "What's my total, all-in cost — and how much of it reaches customers?"

The first question cuts through the most common source of regret: not understanding what you're actually paying. Get the total in real dollars, per month and per year — not a percentage, not "it depends." Then push further: how much of that reaches customers versus covering management and overhead?

If the answer is clear and quick, good. If it's evasive or buried in complexity, you've learned something important before committing a cent. The willingness to state a plain number is one of the best early signals of how a provider will treat you.

2. "What exactly is included — and what will cost extra?"

Regret often comes from the gap between what you assumed "full-service" meant and what the contract actually covers. So map the boundary before you sign. What's included in the price? What counts as an add-on? Extra creative, an additional channel, more detailed reporting, a busier month — do these cost more?

A base price that looked competitive can become expensive once the exclusions surface. Knowing the boundary up front lets you compare honestly and avoid the drip of surprise charges later.

3. "Who actually does the work, and are they specialists?"

The people who sell you the contract are not the people who'll run your campaigns. Ask who will actually be doing the work day to day, and whether they're genuine specialists in the channels you need. You want to know whether your account gets real expertise or gets filed into a queue.

This question also surfaces whether a provider can truly cover all your channels well, or whether they're strong in one area and stretched thin in the rest — which would leave you needing other vendors and back in the fragmentation trap.

4. "What happens to my accounts and data if I leave?"

This is the question almost nobody asks and many later wish they had. Before you sign, understand what you'd keep if the relationship ended. Do your ad accounts, your audience data, and your marketing history belong to your business, or to the provider?

If leaving would mean starting over from scratch — losing the accounts and history you paid to build — you're being quietly locked in, and you should know that before signing, not after. A trustworthy provider is comfortable with you owning your own assets. Resistance here is a meaningful warning.

5. "How do I exit, and what am I committing to?"

Finally, understand the shape of the commitment itself. How long is the term? How does notice work if you want to leave? Are you locked in for a year, or free to go if it's not working?

You're not being pessimistic by asking — you're being prudent. And a provider's answer is revealing: those confident they'll earn your business make leaving easy, because they don't expect you'll want to. Those who trap you with long terms and hard exits are telling you how confident they are that you'd stay by choice.

What the answers tell you

Notice that these five questions aren't really about marketing tactics. They're about how a provider deals with you — on cost, on scope, on expertise, on your ownership, on your freedom. That's deliberate. The tactical stuff you can't fully evaluate up front, but how straight a provider is with you on these five points is highly predictive of the whole relationship.

Ask all five. Clear, comfortable, quick answers across the board are a strong green light. Evasion on any of them marks exactly where the trouble is likely to live.

The contract worth signing

The best providers welcome these questions, because their answers are genuinely reassuring. Clear all-in pricing. A well-defined scope with no nasty exclusions. Real specialists doing the work. Your ownership of your own accounts and data. A fair, easy exit. When a provider meets all five comfortably, you can sign with confidence instead of hope.
