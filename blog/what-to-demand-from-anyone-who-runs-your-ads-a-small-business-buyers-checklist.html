Blog/Choosing a partner

When you hand your marketing to someone else — an agency, a freelancer, a consolidated partner — you're trusting them with real money and a real piece of your business's future. Yet most small business owners approach that decision with less scrutiny than they'd apply to buying a used truck. They go on a good sales pitch, a friendly rep, and a reasonable-sounding quote, and hope it works out.

Factor42 Media4 min read

You can do better, and it doesn't take marketing expertise. It just takes knowing what to demand. Here's the checklist — the standards any provider running your ads should meet, whatever their size or model.

1. Demand a clear, all-in price

You should know exactly what you're paying, in dollars, per month and per year — not just a percentage or a vague "it depends." Demand to know how much of your payment goes to actually reaching customers versus covering the provider's management and overhead. If they can't or won't give you a straight number, that evasion tells you something important before you've signed anything.

2. Demand to know who's actually doing the work

Sales calls are handled by salespeople. Your campaigns won't be. Ask who will actually run your marketing day to day, and whether they're genuine specialists in the channels you care about. You're entitled to know whether your account will get real expertise or be filed into a queue and half-managed by whoever's free.

3. Demand coverage of the channels you actually need

Your customers aren't all in one place, so a provider who only handles one slice — just search, or just social — leaves you back in the fragmentation trap, needing more vendors to fill the gaps. Demand to know the full range of channels they can run, and whether they can grow with you as your needs expand. One provider covering everything beats several each covering a piece.

4. Demand one clear view of results

You should be able to see how your marketing is performing across every channel, in one place, measured consistently — without logging into six platforms or waiting for a vendor to assemble it. Demand a single, honest report that answers the only question that matters: what's actually working? If reporting is scattered or opaque, you'll never really know whether you're getting value.

5. Demand ownership of your own accounts and data

This one protects your future. Wherever possible, your ad accounts, your audience data, and your marketing history should belong to your business — so that if you ever switch providers, you keep what you've built. Demand clarity on this up front. A trustworthy provider has no problem with you owning your own assets; one who resists is quietly locking you in.

6. Demand a real point of contact

When you have a question, you should be able to reach a human who actually knows your account — not a support queue, not a rotating cast of reps who've never seen your business. Demand to know who your point of contact will be and how you reach them. Marketing is too important to run through a ticketing system.

7. Demand honesty about results

No honest provider promises guaranteed results or overnight miracles — marketing doesn't work that way. What you should demand instead is realistic expectations, straight talk about what's working and what isn't, and a willingness to adjust. A provider who over-promises in the sales process will under-deliver in the relationship. Sober honesty is a better sign than a dazzling guarantee.

8. Demand a fair exit

You should never feel trapped. Demand to understand the terms — how notice works, what happens to your accounts and data if you leave, whether you're locked into a long commitment. The confidence a provider shows about letting you leave easily is a good measure of how confident they are that you won't want to.

Using the checklist

You don't need to interrogate a provider aggressively. You just need to work through these eight points and notice how they respond. Clear, quick, comfortable answers are a green light. Evasion, vagueness, or discomfort on any of them is worth taking seriously — those are the areas where problems hide.

Run this checklist against your current provider too, if you have one. Plenty of small businesses discover they're not getting several of these basics, and never thought to demand them.

The provider that welcomes the checklist

Here's the useful thing about a checklist like this: the right provider is glad you have it. Clear pricing, real specialists, full-channel coverage, one honest report, your ownership of your data, a real contact, straight talk, and a fair exit — a provider built to do right by small businesses meets these standards happily, because meeting them is the whole point.
