Competitive Conquesting: Winning Customers From the Business Next Door

Factor42 Media4 min read

Some of the best potential customers for your business are already customers of your competitors. They've demonstrated they want what you offer — they're just buying it from someone else. Competitive conquesting is the practice of reaching those people and making the case that they'd be better off with you. For a small business looking to grow, it's one of the more direct ways to find ready-made customers. Here's what it is and how to use it well.

What competitive conquesting means

Competitive conquesting is advertising aimed at your competitors' customers — reaching people who are already in the market for what you sell, as demonstrated by their interest in or connection to businesses like yours, and presenting your business as the better choice.

The logic is appealing. Rather than trying to create demand from scratch or reach people who may have no interest, you're targeting people who have already shown they want your type of product or service. The demand already exists; you're simply competing to be the one who fills it. It's a way of fishing where the fish already are.

Why it's effective

Conquesting works because it targets intent that already exists. Consider the alternative: much advertising tries to reach broad audiences in the hope of finding the few who are interested. Conquesting starts from a pool of people who are demonstrably interested — they're engaging with your competitors, which means they want what your category offers. Your only job is to win them over, not to convince them they have a need.

For a small business, this efficiency is valuable. Your limited budget goes toward people with proven interest rather than being spread across a broad, mostly-uninterested audience. You're competing for customers at the moment they're actually in the market, which is when advertising has the most influence. Done well, it's a high-return use of ad dollars.

How businesses reach competitors' customers

There are several approaches to reaching the people connected to your competitors — including location-based methods that reach people who visit competitor locations, and audience-targeting methods that reach people showing interest in competing businesses or your category. The specifics matter less than the principle: modern digital advertising makes it possible to reach people based on signals of competitive interest, and to put your message in front of them at a relevant moment.

The important part isn't just reaching them, though — it's what you say when you do. Conquesting only works if you give those customers a genuine reason to choose you: a better offer, a clearer value, a compelling difference. Reaching a competitor's customer with a weak message accomplishes nothing. The targeting opens the door; your value proposition has to walk through it.

Using conquesting the right way

Competitive conquesting is a powerful tactic, but like any single tactic, it delivers its full value as part of a coordinated marketing effort rather than an isolated maneuver. Reaching a competitor's customer once is a start; the real results come when that reach is reinforced across your other channels — the person you reached through conquesting then encountering your business again on social, in search, and elsewhere, building the familiarity and trust that actually wins them over.

That reinforcement requires your channels to work together, which is exactly what a consolidated marketing operation provides and a fragmented one prevents. Run conquesting in isolation and you get a single touch; run it as one coordinated channel among many and you get a sustained, persuasive presence that can genuinely move a customer from a competitor to you. The tactic is only as strong as the coordinated marketing around it.

Win the customers already in the market

Your competitors' customers are proof that demand for what you offer exists — and competitive conquesting is how you compete for that ready-made demand directly. Used well, with a genuine reason to switch and coordinated across your marketing, it's an efficient way for a small business to grow by winning customers who are already looking.
