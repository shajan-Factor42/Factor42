Blog/Choosing a partner

You're paying a marketing provider every month. But are you actually getting your money's worth? For a lot of small business owners, this is an uncomfortable question, because they genuinely don't know. The provider sends reports full of numbers, the invoices keep coming, and yet whether the marketing is truly *working* remains murky. Knowing how to judge whether your provider is delivering is one of the most important — and most overlooked — skills of being a marketing buyer. Here's how to tell.

Factor42 Media4 min read

Why it's so hard to tell

First, some reassurance: if you're unsure whether your provider is working, it's often not because you're missing something obvious. It's because the way marketing gets reported frequently makes real performance hard to see.

Providers often report on metrics that sound impressive but don't clearly connect to your business — impressions, clicks, activity — without making it clear whether any of it is actually growing your business. And when your marketing is spread across several providers, no one shows you the whole picture, so you can't judge the overall result. The murkiness is often built into the reporting, which is exactly why so many owners can't tell whether they're getting value. The first step is knowing what to actually look for.

What to actually look for

Cut through the noise by focusing on what genuinely indicates a provider is working:

Real business results, not vanity metrics. The question isn't whether you got impressions or clicks — it's whether you're getting more of what actually matters to your business: customers, leads, calls, sales. A provider that's working can connect their efforts to real outcomes, not just activity.

Progress toward your actual goals. What did you hire this provider to achieve? A provider that's working can show progress toward those specific goals, not a pile of unrelated numbers.

Clarity, not confusion. A provider that's delivering makes their performance clear and understandable. One that buries results in confusing metrics may be hiding that the results aren't there.

Honest communication. A working provider talks straight about what's working and what isn't, and adjusts. One that only ever reports good news, or goes vague when pressed, is a warning sign.

If you focus on real business results and honest clarity rather than impressive-sounding activity, you can cut through a lot of the fog.

The questions to ask your provider

You can put your provider to the test directly with a few questions:

  • "How is this actually growing my business?" — A working provider connects their work to real outcomes. Vagueness here is telling.
  • "Are we hitting the goals we set?" — A working provider knows your goals and can speak to progress. If they don't reference clear goals, that's a gap.
  • "What's working, what isn't, and what are you changing?" — A working provider is honest about both and actively adjusting. Only-good-news is a red flag.
  • "Can I see how everything's performing together?" — A working provider can show you a clear, whole picture. If they can't, you're flying blind.
  • How readily and clearly a provider answers these tells you a great deal about whether they're truly delivering.

Why you can't judge what you can't see

Here's the deeper issue many small businesses hit: you often can't tell whether your marketing is working because you can't actually see it clearly. When your marketing is scattered across multiple providers, each reporting separately in their own way, assembling a true picture of overall performance is nearly impossible. You get fragments that don't add up to an answer, and the fragmentation itself hides whether you're getting value.

Judging whether your marketing works requires seeing it whole — all channels, measured consistently, in one clear view of real business results. That clarity is hard to get from a fragmented setup and natural from a consolidated one. When one team runs everything and reports it in one honest view, whether your marketing is working becomes visible rather than mysterious. The ability to tell if your marketing is working is, itself, one of the benefits of running it as a coordinated whole.

Demand to know

You deserve to know whether your marketing is actually working, in terms of real business results, not impressive-sounding activity. Focus on outcomes over vanity metrics, progress toward your goals, honest clarity, and a whole-picture view — and demand answers from your provider. If you can't get them, that difficulty is itself the answer.
