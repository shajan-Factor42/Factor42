Five Questions Your Media Plan Must Answer Before You Spend a Dollar

Factor42 Media4 min read

A media plan is just a fancy term for a simple idea: the plan for how you'll spend your marketing budget — which channels, how much on each, aimed at whom, to what end. Every business has one, whether they've written it down or not. The difference is that a good media plan is deliberate and answers hard questions up front, while a bad one is really just a collection of guesses you find out about after the money's gone.

Before you spend a single dollar on advertising, your media plan should have clear answers to five questions. If it can't answer them, you're not investing your budget — you're gambling it. Here they are.

1. Who exactly are we trying to reach?

Everything starts here, and a surprising number of media plans skip it. Not "everyone who might buy," but a specific, honest picture of your actual best customers — who they are, where they are, what they care about, and where they spend their attention.

This answer drives every other decision. You can't choose the right channels or craft the right message until you know precisely who's on the other end. A plan that hasn't nailed down its target is destined to spray budget at the wrong people. If your media plan can't clearly state who it's reaching, stop and answer that before anything else.

2. What are we actually trying to achieve?

A media plan needs a real goal, stated concretely — not "more marketing" or "get our name out there," but a specific outcome the spending is meant to produce. More phone calls? Store visits? Online orders? Leads for a service? Different goals call for different channels, messages, and measures of success.

Without a clear objective, you can't tell whether the plan is working, because you never defined what working looks like. The goal is the yardstick everything else is measured against. If your plan doesn't name what it's trying to achieve, it can't be judged a success or failure — which usually means budget quietly wasted.

3. Which channels, and why those?

Now the plan gets practical: where will the budget actually go? Search, social, streaming TV, email, programmatic, and the rest each reach people differently and suit different goals and audiences. The right mix depends entirely on your answers to the first two questions.

The critical word here is why. Each channel in the plan should be there for a reason connected to your audience and goal — not because it's familiar, or because someone sold you on it. A plan that can justify each channel's place is deliberate. A plan that includes channels out of habit is leaking budget into places that may not serve its purpose.

4. How much goes where — and is it coordinated?

Beyond which channels, the plan has to answer how the budget divides among them, and — crucially — whether they'll work together. Splitting money across channels is one thing; coordinating them so they reinforce each other is what separates a real plan from a scattered one.

This is where fragmented setups fall apart. When channels are run by separate vendors, the budget gets divided but never coordinated — each channel operates blind to the others, and the plan is really several disconnected mini-plans. A strong media plan treats the channels as one coordinated effort, with budget flowing to what works and the channels reinforcing each other. Ask whether your plan is one coordinated whole or just a pile of separate spends.

5. How will we know if it's working?

Finally, before you spend, the plan must define how you'll measure success and see results. What will you track? How will you know which channels are performing? Where will the results actually show up so you can act on them?

A plan with no measurement built in is a plan you can't learn from — you'll spend the budget and never really know what it bought. And measurement has to span all the channels together, in one view, or you'll only ever see disconnected fragments. If your plan can't tell you how you'll know it's working, it can't improve, and you'll repeat the same guesses next time.

A plan, not a gamble

Five questions: who, what, which channels and why, how much and coordinated how, and how you'll measure. A media plan that answers all five is a real investment strategy. A plan that can't is a gamble dressed up as a plan — and small businesses can't afford to gamble their limited budgets.

Notice how many of the five point toward coordination and unified measurement — reaching the right people across the right channels, working together, measured as a whole. That's hard to achieve when your marketing is scattered across separate vendors, and natural when it's run as one operation.
