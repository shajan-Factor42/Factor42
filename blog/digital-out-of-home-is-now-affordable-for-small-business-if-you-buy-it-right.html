Blog/Choosing a partner

The billboard has always been the symbol of advertising you couldn't afford. Big, expensive, reserved for major brands, locked up in long contracts — a classic small-business no-go. But out-of-home advertising has gone digital, and in doing so it has quietly become accessible to businesses that could never have considered it before. Digital out-of-home — DOOH — puts your business on screens in the physical world, affordably and flexibly, *if you buy it the right way.* Here's what it is and how to approach it.

Factor42 Media4 min read

What DOOH actually is

Digital out-of-home is advertising on digital screens in physical spaces — the screens you pass in shopping centers, gyms, offices, transit stops, gas stations, elevators, restaurants, and along roadways. It's the modern, digital evolution of the old static billboard and poster.

The "digital" part is what changes everything. Because these are digital screens served through digital systems, DOOH can be bought and targeted the way online advertising is — by location, by time of day, flexibly and in smaller increments — rather than through the expensive, rigid, long-term contracts that defined traditional outdoor advertising. That flexibility is exactly what brings it within a small business's reach.

Why it's affordable now — with a catch

DOOH has become accessible to small businesses because the digital buying model removes the old barriers. You don't need to commit to an enormous, long-term billboard contract. You can target specific screens in specific places at specific times, buying the exposure that's relevant to you without paying for a fortune's worth you don't need. The cost of reaching people on physical-world screens has come down to earth.

But there's a real catch in the "if you buy it right." DOOH is affordable and effective when it's bought smartly and targeted well — the right screens, the right locations, the right timing, coordinated with the rest of your marketing. Bought carelessly, any channel wastes money, and DOOH is no exception. The affordability is real, but capturing it requires buying with some know-how rather than just putting an ad on a screen and hoping. This is the difference between DOOH working for a small business and DOOH being a costly novelty.

What DOOH does that screens-in-hand can't

DOOH offers something your online channels fundamentally can't: it reaches people in the physical world, in public, with an ad they can't scroll past, close, or block.

While so much advertising fights for attention on personal screens that people are actively trying to ignore, DOOH meets people out in the world — near where they shop, work, and move through their day. Consumers tend to view these ads relatively favorably compared to more intrusive formats, and for a local business, a screen near your location or in the areas your customers frequent can drive real awareness and foot traffic. It reaches customers in the moments they're not on their phones, which is a genuinely different and complementary kind of presence.

Why buying it right means buying it coordinated

The key to DOOH delivering its value — buying it right — points directly to running it as part of a coordinated marketing effort rather than a standalone billboard buy. When DOOH is targeted thoughtfully and coordinated with your other channels, it becomes far more powerful: the awareness it builds in the physical world can be reinforced by your online channels, and the people who encounter you on a screen can be reached again through search, social, and the rest.

That coordination and smart buying are exactly what a consolidated marketing operation provides. Rather than figuring out DOOH alone and risking a careless buy, it becomes one channel in a unified plan — bought well, targeted properly, coordinated with everything else, and measured as part of the whole. That's how a small business captures DOOH's affordability and impact instead of wasting money on screens.

Put your business in the physical world

Digital out-of-home has opened the physical-world screen to small businesses — affordable, flexible, and capable of reaching people where online ads can't. The opportunity is real, provided you buy it right: targeted well and coordinated with the rest of your marketing rather than bought blind.
