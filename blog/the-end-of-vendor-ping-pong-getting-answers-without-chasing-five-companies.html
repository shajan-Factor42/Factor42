Blog/Choosing a partner

You've probably played this game, even if you've never named it. Something's off with your marketing — leads dropped, a campaign looks wrong, a number doesn't add up — and you set out to get an answer. You ask your search vendor. They say it's not on their end; check with whoever runs your social. You ask the social agency. They point you toward your website person. The website person suspects it's an email issue. Around and around it goes, each company confident the problem lives somewhere else, and you're the ball being knocked between them.

Factor42 Media4 min read

This is vendor ping-pong, and it's one of the most maddening features of running marketing across multiple providers. It wastes your time, delays every answer, and leaves real problems unsolved while everyone insists it's not their department. Here's why it happens and how to make it stop.

Why ping-pong is built into fragmentation

Vendor ping-pong isn't caused by bad or lazy providers. It's the natural result of splitting one connected system among parties who each see only a piece of it.

When your marketing runs across several vendors, each one genuinely can only see their own slice. So when you bring them a problem that might span channels, each can honestly say "it doesn't appear to be us" — because from where they sit, looking only at their piece, it isn't. Nobody's lying. But nobody can see the whole picture either, so nobody can actually diagnose a problem that lives between channels or requires seeing all of them at once. The question bounces because there's no one positioned to catch it.

The structure guarantees the ping-pong. As long as your marketing is fragmented, any cross-channel question will get knocked around, because no single vendor has the vantage point to answer it.

What the ping-pong actually costs

The game is more than annoying. It carries real costs that add up.

Time, obviously. Every round of "check with the other vendor" is an email, a wait, a follow-up. A question that should take one conversation takes days and a dozen messages. Your time — the scarcest thing you have — gets poured into being the go-between.

Delayed answers, delayed fixes. While the question bounces, the underlying problem persists. If a campaign is misfiring or budget is leaking, every day of ping-pong is a day the issue keeps costing you. Slow diagnosis means slow resolution, and slow resolution is expensive.

Problems that never get solved at all. The worst outcome is the common one: the question bounces until you give up. Exhausted from chasing five companies, you just stop asking, and the problem quietly continues. Cross-channel issues that no single vendor owns tend to become permanent, because solving them requires a coordination that a fragmented setup can't provide.

Your energy. There's a real toll in being the perpetual middleman, translating between vendors and refereeing their "not us" responses. It makes running your marketing feel like a chore, which is exactly what it shouldn't be.

The fix: one team that can't play ping-pong

The end of vendor ping-pong comes from removing the thing that causes it — the fragmentation. When one team runs every channel from one place, there's no one to bounce the question to. There's no "other vendor" to point at, no slice someone can't see, no gap for the problem to hide in.

You bring a question — any question, spanning any channels — to your single point of contact, and they can actually answer it, because they see your whole marketing at once. If something's misfiring, they can diagnose it across the entire operation rather than squinting at one slice. If a problem lives between channels, there's no "between" anymore, because it's all one connected system under one team. The question stops, and the answer starts.

That's the structural cure. Ping-pong requires multiple players who each see only part of the court. Consolidate to one team seeing the whole court, and the game simply can't be played.

Get your answers back

You shouldn't have to chase five companies to find out what's happening with your own marketing. You shouldn't be the ball. And you certainly shouldn't have to give up on real problems because no vendor will own them. Getting a straight answer to a straight question is the baseline you deserve — and fragmentation quietly takes it away.

Factor42 Media ends the ping-pong by running your entire marketing operation from one consolidated place, with one team and one point of contact who can see and answer for everything. Bring any question about any part of your marketing, and get a real answer from someone who can actually see the whole picture — no bouncing, no "check with the other vendor," no unsolved problems slipping through the cracks.

Stop being the ball. Get one team that can answer for all of it.
