Blog/Channels & platforms

Retail media — advertising on the platforms and websites where people shop, like online marketplaces and retailer sites — has become one of the fastest-growing areas in all of advertising. Much of the conversation around it, though, is aimed at huge brands with enormous budgets, which can leave a smaller business assuming it's not for them. It can be. Winning in retail media doesn't require an enterprise budget — it requires being smart and efficient with the budget you have. Here's how a smaller business can compete.

Factor42 Media4 min read

What retail media is, briefly

Retail media is advertising placed where people shop — within online retailers, marketplaces, and shopping platforms — reaching customers at the point of buying rather than while they're doing something else. When a product appears as sponsored on a shopping site or marketplace, that's retail media.

Its appeal is the context: you reach people who are actively shopping, often ready to buy, right where the purchase happens. That proximity to the actual moment of purchase is what makes retail media so effective and so fast-growing — and it's just as valuable at a small scale as a large one.

Why small businesses assume it's out of reach — and why they're wrong

The retail media conversation is dominated by big brands spending big budgets, which creates an impression that it's an enterprise game. But that impression confuses how much some businesses spend with what it takes to benefit. Retail media, like most digital advertising, can be entered at modest budgets and made effective through smart targeting and efficiency rather than sheer spending.

A smaller business doesn't win retail media by outspending big brands — it can't and shouldn't try. It wins by being efficient: reaching the right shoppers, in the right categories, with a budget spent wisely rather than sprayed broadly. The enterprise-budget assumption keeps smaller businesses out of an opportunity they could actually capture. Don't let the big-brand noise convince you it's not for you.

How to win retail media on a smaller budget

Competing in retail media without enterprise resources comes down to efficiency and focus:

Be precise about where you compete. Rather than trying to be everywhere, focus on the specific categories and products where you can genuinely win. A focused presence beats a thin, scattered one.

Target the right shoppers. Reach the people most likely to buy what you offer, so your limited budget isn't wasted on broad, irrelevant reach. Precision is how a small budget competes.

Spend where it works and adjust. Watch what's performing and move your budget toward it, compounding returns over time rather than setting and forgetting.

Coordinate with your other marketing. Retail media captures purchase intent, but your broader marketing builds the awareness that makes shoppers choose you. Together they're far stronger.

None of this requires a big budget — it requires running retail media smartly, as part of a coordinated whole.

Why coordination is the smaller business's edge

Here's where a smaller business can actually gain an advantage: coordination. A big brand throwing budget at retail media in isolation is still leaving value on the table if that spending doesn't connect to the rest of its marketing. A smaller business that coordinates its retail media with its other channels — building awareness through search, social, and streaming, then capturing purchase intent through retail media — makes every dollar work harder through reinforcement.

That coordination is exactly what running your marketing as one operation provides. Retail media as one connected channel — where the demand your other channels create gets captured at the point of purchase — outperforms retail media run as an isolated, disconnected effort. For a smaller business, this smart coordination is how you compete with bigger players who may be spending more but coordinating less. Efficiency and integration beat raw budget.

Compete smart, not big

Retail media is a powerful, fast-growing opportunity, and it's not reserved for enterprise budgets. A smaller business can win by being efficient and focused — reaching the right shoppers, spending where it works, and coordinating retail media with the rest of its marketing so every dollar counts. You don't need to outspend the big brands; you need to out-smart them.
