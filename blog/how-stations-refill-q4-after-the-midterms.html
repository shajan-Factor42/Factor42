After the Midterms: How TV and Radio Stations Refill Q4 With Digital

Factor42 MediaNov 8, 20265 min read

For most TV and radio stations, the weeks before Election Day are the busiest of the year. Political buyers take every spot they can get, local advertisers get squeezed out of their usual schedules, and sales teams spend the fall managing scarcity. Then the election ends, the political money leaves almost overnight, and the rest of Q4 has to be sold again.

The stations that refill Q4 fastest give their sellers a full digital menu to bring back to local advertisers, alongside airtime. This guide covers what changes after Election Day, which digital products sell to local advertisers in the holiday quarter, how sellers package digital with airtime, and how fulfillment and reporting work across markets. For the broader model, see how Factor42 supports TV and radio groups.

What changes for stations after Election Day?

After Election Day, political spend stops almost immediately, and stations have to replace that revenue with local advertisers for the rest of the fourth quarter. The inventory that political campaigns held in October is suddenly open again in November.

Three things usually happen at once:

  • Political flights end, and with them a large share of the quarter's revenue.
  • Local advertisers who were preempted or priced out during the campaign return, often with holiday budgets to spend.
  • Sales teams switch from managing demand to creating it, with only a few weeks before the holidays.

The opportunity is that many local advertisers spent the fall unable to get the schedules they wanted. When sellers come back with a plan that combines airtime with digital, those advertisers are ready to talk.

Which digital products sell to local advertisers in Q4?

The digital products that sell best to local advertisers in Q4 are the ones that extend a station's audience into streaming and catch holiday shoppers when they are ready to buy: streaming TV, streaming audio, paid search, paid social and programmatic display. Each one fills a specific gap next to broadcast.

What each product adds for a local advertiser:

  • Streaming TV (OTT/CTV) reaches viewers who watch through connected TVs rather than over the air.
  • Streaming and podcast audio extends a radio schedule to listeners on apps and smart speakers.
  • Paid search captures people already searching for what the advertiser sells.
  • Paid social reaches local audiences by interest and location on Facebook and Instagram.
  • Programmatic display and pre-roll keep the advertiser in front of people who saw the broadcast spot.
  • Digital out-of-home adds screens near stores, malls and busy roads during holiday traffic.
  • Email marketing gives retailers a direct channel for holiday offers.

Not every advertiser needs every product. A retailer running a holiday sale gets the most from search, social and retargeting around the event dates. An auto dealer with a year-end sales event may lead with streaming TV and search. A health system promoting open enrollment may lean on streaming TV and audio. The point of a full menu is that sellers can fit the plan to the advertiser instead of selling whatever the station happens to fulfill in-house.

How do sellers package digital with airtime?

Sellers package digital with airtime by pairing each broadcast schedule with the digital products that extend it, so the advertiser buys one plan for one goal instead of separate line items. The broadcast spot builds awareness, and the digital pieces add reach and capture the response.

Packages that work well in Q4:

  • TV plus streaming TV: the same creative on broadcast and connected TV, reaching viewers who no longer watch over the air.
  • Radio plus streaming audio: the same spot on air and in streaming apps, covering listeners wherever they tune in.
  • Broadcast plus search and social: airtime drives awareness, while search and social catch people who look the advertiser up afterward.
  • Event packages: a short, heavy flight around a holiday sale or year-end event, with retargeting running through the event date.

Simple tiers help sellers move quickly. A starter package might add one digital product to an existing schedule, while a fuller package adds three or four around a holiday flight. Keeping the order form the same for every package lets sellers build a proposal in minutes rather than calling ad ops for every quote.

Who fulfills the orders?

The orders can be fulfilled by the station's own digital team, by separate vendors for each product, or by one white-label partner that runs every product under the station's brand. For most groups, the deciding factor is whether fulfillment can keep up when Q4 orders arrive all at once in every market.

In-house teams work well when a station has the staff and skills for each product, but few stations have specialists for streaming TV, search, social and programmatic in every market. Separate vendors cover more products but bring separate order forms, timelines and reports. A single white-label partner gives every market the same workflow: sellers submit one order, the partner builds and launches every product it calls for, and the advertiser sees only the station's brand.

Whichever route a group chooses, the questions are the same: can it handle a surge, does every market get the same quality, and does the station keep ownership of its advertiser relationships and accounts? Our guide on when to outsource ad operations covers the signs that a station group has outgrown its current setup, and Q4 ad ops without the burnout covers planning for the holiday surge.

How is it reported by market?

Digital campaigns should be reported by market and by product, showing budget against spend and delivery for every advertiser, so sales managers can compare markets and sellers can walk into renewals with clear numbers. Station-branded wrap reports keep the advertiser relationship with the station.

Good market-level reporting includes:

  • Budget versus spend for each market and product.
  • Delivery for each campaign, with pacing visible while it runs.
  • Monthly rollups across every advertiser and flight.
  • Booked budget for future flights, so managers can see what is coming in December and January.
  • Wrap reports with the station's branding for each advertiser.

This matters most at renewal time. An advertiser who added streaming TV or search in November will ask in January what it delivered. A seller with a clear, branded report for that market has a renewal conversation; a seller without one has a cancellation.

The bottom line

Political spend leaves after Election Day, but local advertisers come back with holiday budgets and a backlog of plans they could not run in October. Stations that offer a full digital menu next to airtime, fulfill it consistently in every market and report it by market turn that moment into Q4 revenue and better renewals in the new year.
